How to read the pronunciationRead it as English and stress the syllable in CAPITALS; always pronounce the r (lightly rolled or in the throat) and say w halfway between v and w. kh = the rasping 'ch' of Scottish 'loch' (Dutch g/ch); uh or u = a short weak 'uh' (so uhr = 'uh' + r); ai = 'eye', never 'ay' (ij/ei); ow = 'ow' as in 'now', but in OWT (ui) say it with rounded lips; ur = the vowel of 'fur' with rounded lips and no r at all (eu); ew = 'ee' said with rounded lips, like French 'tu' (uu); oo = 'oo' as in 'too' (oe).
  • Hoeveel kost het?How much is it?hoo-VAYL kost ut
  • Mag ik pinnen?Can I pay by card?mahkh ik PIN-nuhpinnen = paying by card (from the PIN code). The answer is almost always yes; debit cards work everywhere, and a few small places still refuse credit cards.
  • Kan ik contant betalen?Can I pay cash?kahn ik kon-TAHNT buh-TAH-luh
  • Ik kijk even rondI'm just lookingik kaik AY-vuh rontHandy when staff ask 'Kan ik u helpen?' (Can I help you?).
  • Mag ik een tasje?Can I have a bag?mahkh ik un TAH-shuhBags cost extra; most people bring their own.
  • Mag ik de bon?Can I have the receipt?mahkh ik duh BONYou'll often be asked 'Wil je de bon?' or 'Bonnetje erbij?' – answer 'Ja, graag' or 'Nee, dank je'.
  • Mag ik dit passen?Can I try this on?mahkh ik dit PAH-suh
  • Deze, graagThis one, pleaseDAY-zuh, khrahkhStrictly it's 'dit' for 'het' words, but pointing and saying 'deze' is fine.
  • Hoe laat gaat u dicht?What time do you close?hoo LAHT khaht ew DIKHTMany shops close around 18:00, but stay open until 21:00 on one late-shopping evening (koopavond, usually Thursday or Friday). Supermarkets often open until 21:00 or 22:00; Sunday hours vary by town.

💡 Card is king: some shops and cafés don't take cash at all ('Alleen pin' = card only).
