How to read the pronunciationRead it as English and stress the syllable in CAPITALS; always pronounce the r (lightly rolled or in the throat) and say w halfway between v and w. kh = the rasping 'ch' of Scottish 'loch' (Dutch g/ch); uh or u = a short weak 'uh' (so uhr = 'uh' + r); ai = 'eye', never 'ay' (ij/ei); ow = 'ow' as in 'now', but in OWT (ui) say it with rounded lips; ur = the vowel of 'fur' with rounded lips and no r at all (eu); ew = 'ee' said with rounded lips, like French 'tu' (uu); oo = 'oo' as in 'too' (oe).
  • Een retourtje naar …, alsjeblieftA return ticket to …, pleaseun ruh-TOOR-chuh nahr …, ahl-shuh-BLEEFTOne-way is 'een enkeltje'. Visitors usually buy tickets at the machine or simply check in with a contactless bank card.
  • Inchecken … uitcheckenCheck in … check outIN-chek-kuh … OWT-chek-kuhHold your card (contactless bank card or OV-pas) to the reader when you get on and again when you get off. Forget to check out and you pay a fixed fare: €4 on buses, trams and metros, up to €20 on the train.
  • Kan ik met mijn bankpas inchecken?Can I check in with my bank card?kahn ik met main BAHNK-pahs IN-chek-kuhYes: contactless debit and credit cards, and cards in your phone or watch, work on all trains, trams, buses and metros. Check out with the same card or device – the card in your phone counts as a different card from the plastic one.
  • Gaat deze trein naar …?Does this train go to …?khaht DAY-zuh TRAIN nahr …
  • Van welk spoor vertrekt de trein?Which platform does the train leave from?vahn welk SPOHR vuhr-TREKT duh trainspoor = platform. Watch out for 5a and 5b: same platform, different ends.
  • Volgende halte: …Next stop: …VOL-khun-duh HAHL-tuh …
  • Waar is de bushalte?Where is the bus stop?wahr is duh BUHS-hahl-tuh
  • De trein heeft vertragingThe train is delayedduh train hayft vuhr-TRAH-khingOn departure boards you may also see 'Rijdt niet' (cancelled).
  • Waar kan ik een fiets huren?Where can I rent a bike?wahr kahn ik un FEETS HEW-ruhLook for the sign 'fietsverhuur' (bike rental).

💡 Look for 'Stilte' (silence) signs on trains: no phone calls or loud talking in those sections.
