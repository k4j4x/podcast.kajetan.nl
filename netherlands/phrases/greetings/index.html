How to read the pronunciationRead it as English and stress the syllable in CAPITALS; always pronounce the r (lightly rolled or in the throat) and say w halfway between v and w. kh = the rasping 'ch' of Scottish 'loch' (Dutch g/ch); uh or u = a short weak 'uh' (so uhr = 'uh' + r); ai = 'eye', never 'ay' (ij/ei); ow = 'ow' as in 'now', but in OWT (ui) say it with rounded lips; ur = the vowel of 'fur' with rounded lips and no r at all (eu); ew = 'ee' said with rounded lips, like French 'tu' (uu); oo = 'oo' as in 'too' (oe).
  • Hallo / HoiHello / Hihah-LOH / HOYHoi is casual and very common; Hallo works everywhere.
  • GoedemorgenGood morningkhoo-duh-MOR-khuh
  • GoedemiddagGood afternoonkhoo-duh-MID-dahkh
  • GoedenavondGood eveningkhoo-duh-NAH-vont
  • Hoe gaat het?How are you?hoo KHAHT ut
  • Goed, dank je! En met jou?Fine, thanks! And you?KHOOT, dahnk yuh! en met YOWCasual. Polite version: 'Goed, dank u. En met u?'
  • Leuk je te ontmoetenNice to meet youLURK yuh tuh ont-MOO-tuhCasual. When shaking hands formally, people just say 'Aangenaam'.
  • Gezellig!Cosy! / What a nice time!khuh-ZEL-ukhThe famous untranslatable Dutch word: cosy, convivial, a good time together. Use it for a café, an evening or a person.
  • Doei! / Dag!Bye!DOOY / DAHKHDoei is casual (often 'Doei doei!'); Dag is neutral and polite, and in shops it can also mean hello.
  • Tot ziens!Goodbye! / See you!tot SEENS

💡 Hoi and Doei are casual; with older strangers, Goedemorgen/Goedemiddag and Dag are safer.
