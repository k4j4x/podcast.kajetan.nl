Raw herring

Hollandse Nieuwe

HOL-lahnt-suh NEE-wuh

Hollandse Nieuwe is young herring, caught in early summer when the fish is at its fattest. It isn’t cooked: it’s gutted the traditional way, lightly salted and left to ripen for a few days, which makes it soft, mild and only slightly salty. By law it is frozen first, so eating it raw is safe.

The new season opens every June, when the first new herring reaches the fish stalls. In Scheveningen the new catch is usually celebrated in June with Vlaggetjesdag (Flag Day), a harbour festival. Officially the name Hollandse Nieuwe may only be used until the end of September, but herring itself is sold all year round.

You buy it at a fish stall or market: on its own, with chopped raw onion and pickles, or as a broodje haring, in a soft white bun.

How to eat it

Hold the herring by its tail, tip your head back and lower it into your mouth, bite by bite. Prefer something tidier? Many stalls will also cut it into pieces and give you a cocktail stick.
