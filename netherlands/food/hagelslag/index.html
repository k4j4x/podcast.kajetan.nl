Hagelslag

HAH-khul-slahkh

Hagelslag (‘hailstorm’) is sprinkles on bread: tiny strands of chocolate, or fruit- or aniseed-flavoured sugar, scattered over a slice of buttered bread. To the Dutch it’s not a cake decoration but an everyday sandwich topping, eaten at breakfast and lunch by children and adults alike. More than 14 million kilos are eaten every year.

The first hagelslag, in 1908, was white and flavoured with aniseed; chocolate came later. Only sprinkles with at least 20% cocoa may be called chocolate hagelslag.

A related tradition: when a baby is born, visitors get beschuit met muisjes, a round, crisp rusk topped with sugar-coated aniseed sprinkles: pink and white for a girl, blue and white for a boy.

How to eat it

Butter the bread first so the sprinkles stick, then scatter on a generous layer and press lightly. Eat it over your plate: hagelslag gets everywhere.
