Limburg vlaai

Limburgse vlaai

LIM-buhrkh-suh VLAH-ee

Limburgse vlaai is a flat, round pie from Limburg, the southernmost province. Its base is a sweet yeast dough, more like bread than shortcrust, filled with fruit such as cherries, apricots, plums or gooseberries, or with rice pudding (rijstevlaai) or custard. Many have a lattice of dough strips or a crumble on top.

In Limburg, vlaai used to be a luxury for feast days, from village fairs to weddings, served with coffee in the afternoon. It’s still the way to show Limburg hospitality, at birthdays and family gatherings. Since 2024 Limburgse vlaai has been a protected EU regional product: only vlaai baked in Limburg (Dutch or Belgian) may carry the name.

You’ll see vlaai in bakeries all over the country, but the classic place to eat it is the south.

How to eat it

The base is firm enough to eat a slice from your hand, the traditional way; at a table a cake fork works fine. Try rijstevlaai, the creamy rice-pudding classic.
