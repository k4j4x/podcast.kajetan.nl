Oliebollen

OH-lee-bol-luh

Oliebollen (‘oil balls’) are deep-fried balls of yeasted dough, usually studded with currants or raisins, sometimes with apple or candied peel, and finished with a thick dusting of powdered sugar. Think of a doughnut without a hole.

They’re the traditional treat for New Year’s Eve (oudejaarsavond), when many households fry their own or buy a big bag. From about early October to early January, oliebollen stalls (oliebollenkramen) pop up in towns and cities, frying them fresh all day; you’ll also find them at fairs during the year.

Their origin is uncertain; they may go back to the Middle Ages.

How to eat it

Eat them warm, straight from the paper bag, and expect a coat of powdered sugar on your jacket. Don’t breathe in as you bite.
