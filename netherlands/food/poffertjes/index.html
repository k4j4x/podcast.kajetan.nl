Poffertjes

POF-fuhr-chus

Poffertjes are tiny, puffy pancakes made from a yeasted batter, traditionally of wheat and buckwheat flour. At a stall they’re cooked on a large copper plate full of small round hollows, and flipped with a fork while still a little runny inside, which makes them round, fluffy and soft in the middle.

You’ll find them at poffertjes stalls at fairs, markets, summer festivals and Christmas markets, and in some pancake houses. A portion is about a dozen, served on a small plate with a knob of butter and lots of powdered sugar.

They’ve been a fairground treat since at least the 18th century.

How to eat it

Let the butter melt, swirl the poffertjes through the butter and sugar, and spear them with the little fork. Eat them hot, before the butter sets.
