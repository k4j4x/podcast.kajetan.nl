Kroket

Broodje kroket

BROH-chuh kroh-KET

The Dutch kroket is a finger-shaped croquette: thick beef or veal ragout, rolled in breadcrumbs and deep-fried. Think of it as the big sister of the bitterbal. The Netherlands eats around 300 million of them a year, about 25 per person.

You’ll find it at every snack bar and in many canteens and lunchrooms, often as a broodje kroket: a kroket in a soft white bun with mustard, a classic quick lunch. Some snack bars also sell them from a wall of small heated compartments, the automatiek: pay, open the little door and take your snack.

Besides the classic beef kroket there are veal, goulash and vegetable versions.

How to eat it

In a bun: press the kroket flat with the top half of the bun, add mustard and eat it like a sandwich. On its own: break it open and give the ragout a moment to cool, because it’s very hot inside.
