Pea soup (snert)

Erwtensoep / snert

ER-tuh-soop / SNERT

Erwtensoep, also called snert, is Dutch split-pea soup, and it’s thick: an old rule of thumb says a wooden spoon should stay upright in the pot. It’s made with green split peas, pork, celeriac or celery, leek, onion and carrot, often with potato, and slices of smoked sausage (rookworst) go in just before serving.

It’s a winter dish. Strictly speaking, snert is soup that has stood overnight to thicken and gain flavour, so many people cook a big pan and eat it for days. On the rare winter days when canals and lakes freeze over, the little koek-en-zopie stalls for skaters usually serve it too.

The traditional side is rye bread (roggebrood) with katenspek, a cooked and smoked bacon.

How to eat it

Eat it piping hot with a spoon, with a bite of rye bread and bacon between spoonfuls. Don’t expect a light starter: one bowl is a meal.
