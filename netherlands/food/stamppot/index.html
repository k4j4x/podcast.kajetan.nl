Stamppot

Stamppot

STAHM-pot

Stamppot is Dutch winter comfort food: potatoes cooked and mashed together with a vegetable. The classics are boerenkool (kale), zuurkool (sauerkraut), andijvie (curly endive, sometimes stirred in raw) and hutspot (carrots and onion). It usually comes with smoked sausage (rookworst) or bacon, and a pool of gravy.

It’s mostly cooked at home on cold days, from autumn into early spring, but in winter you’ll also find it on the menu in traditional cafés and restaurants.

Leiden even has its own hutspot day. On 3 October the city celebrates the end of the Spanish siege of 1574, and hutspot is the traditional meal. According to tradition, a Leiden boy found a pot of it in the abandoned Spanish camp; back then it would have been made with parsnips rather than potatoes.

How to eat it

Make a little well (kuiltje) in the mash, fill it with gravy and mix as you go. Slice the sausage and get a bit of everything on each forkful.
