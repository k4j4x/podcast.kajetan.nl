Stroopwafel

STROHP-wah-ful

A stroopwafel (‘syrup waffle’) is two thin, crisp waffle discs with a sticky syrup filling in between. The dough is baked briefly in a hot waffle iron, cut round and split in half while still warm, then spread with a warm filling of syrup, brown sugar, butter and cinnamon, which sets as it cools.

It comes from Gouda, where bakers started making siroopwafels in the 19th century; exactly when is unclear, because early records are missing. Today packaged stroopwafels are in every supermarket, but the real treat is a fresh one, made in front of you at a market stall and still warm.

Look out for bags of offcuts too: the crumbly edges trimmed off during baking are sold separately.

How to eat it

Rest a stroopwafel on top of your cup of hot coffee or tea for a minute: the steam warms the syrup and softens the waffle. A fresh one from the market is best eaten straight away, while the syrup is still runny.
