Dutch cheese

Kaas

KAHS

Gouda and Edam are the famous ones, both named after the towns where they were traded for centuries. Gouda is a yellow cow’s-milk cheese shaped like a wheel; Edam is a smaller ball, known abroad for its red wax coat. Leidse kaas, from around Leiden, is flavoured with cumin seeds. Cheese labelled ‘Gouda Holland’ or ‘Edam Holland’ must be made in the Netherlands from Dutch milk.

Dutch cheese is sold by age, from jong (young, mild and supple, about 4 weeks) via belegen (matured) to oud (old, firm, with crunchy little crystals, around a year). People eat it in slices on bread at breakfast and lunch, and in cubes with mustard as a snack with a beer.

In summer you can watch traditional cheese markets, mainly a show for visitors today: in Alkmaar on Friday mornings from about late March to September, and in Gouda on Thursday mornings from April to August. Dates change each year, so check before you go.

How to eat it

Not sure which age you like? Ask to taste first; then try old cheese in cubes with mustard. For bread, the Dutch shave thin slices off the block with a cheese slicer (kaasschaaf).
