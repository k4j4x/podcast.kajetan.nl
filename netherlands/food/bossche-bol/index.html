Bossche bol

Bossche bol

BOS-suh BOL

A Bossche bol is a big ball of choux pastry, about 12 cm across, filled with whipped cream and dipped in dark chocolate. It’s the pride of ’s-Hertogenbosch (better known as Den Bosch), the capital of North Brabant.

Its history is a little murky. A Den Bosch baker was already advertising chocolate balls with a custard-cream filling in 1903, and in 1924 offered them with whipped cream too; other bakers in the city have their own claim to the recipe. The name Bossche bol only caught on later, as the pastry became famous outside the city.

You can find them elsewhere in the country, but they’re best fresh in Den Bosch itself.

How to eat it

Hold it with both hands and bite in from the side, or use a knife and fork: the chocolate is kept soft enough not to shatter. Either way, the cream will try to escape, so grab a napkin.
