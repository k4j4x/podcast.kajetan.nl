How to read the pronunciationStress the syllable in CAPITALS. Dutch lines: read them as English and always pronounce the r, rolled or throaty (both are heard in Flanders). kh = the 'ch' of Scottish 'loch' (Dutch g/ch); in Belgium it is softer and voiced, more a gentle gargle than a rasp; uh = a short weak 'uh' (uhr = 'uh' + r); ai = 'eye' (ij/ei); ow = 'ow' as in 'now', said with rounded lips (ui); ur = the vowel of 'fur' with rounded lips and no r (eu); ew = 'ee' said with rounded lips (uu); oo = as in 'too' (oe); w = close to English w, as Flemings say it. French lines: stress lightly on the last syllable; (n) = don't say the n, say the vowel through your nose: ah(n), oh(n), and a(n) like the a of 'bad' but nasal; zh = the 's' of 'measure'; ay = a short 'ay' with no glide; eh = as in 'bed'; ew, oo, ur and uh as above; r = a soft throaty r, always pronounced.
