Ardennes ham

Ardense hamahr-DEN-suh HAHMJambon d'Ardennezhah(n)-boh(n) dahr-DEN

Jambon d’Ardenne is a raw ham made from the pig’s hind leg: salted dry or rubbed with salt or brine, then matured in the cold. Smoking is optional, but if it is smoked, it must be over oak or beech. You find it on the bone, or boned as the smaller ‘cœur’ and ‘noix’ cuts.

The name has been protected in Belgium since 1974 and as an EU protected geographical indication since 1996. That means every step of making it must take place in the Ardennes area, in the province of Luxembourg and neighbouring parts of the provinces of Liège and Namur; the pork itself does not have to come from there.

Records from the early 19th century already mention ham being served to travellers in the Ardennes. Today it is eaten with bread or raw vegetables, with melon, and in Ardennes dishes such as a ham omelette or pancakes.

How to eat it

Eat it with bread, raw vegetables or melon, as a starter or with a drink. In the Ardennes, try it in a ham omelette.
