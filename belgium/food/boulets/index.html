Liège meatballs

Luikse balletjesLOWK-suh BAHL-luh-chusBoulets à la liégeoiseboo-leh ah lah lyay-ZHWAHZ

Boulets à la liégeoise are large meatballs of pork mixed with veal or beef, breadcrumbs, onion and parsley, simmered in a thick sweet-and-sour sauce. The sauce gets its colour and taste from onions, vinegar, brown sugar, currants and above all sirop de Liège, a dark, sticky syrup made by cooking down apple and pear juice. They are called boulets, not boulettes, because they are bigger and more filling.

In Liège they are an institution, served in brasseries and friteries as boulets-frites: with fries and mayonnaise. The city’s tourist office says no visit is complete without them. The sauce is also called sauce lapin, ‘rabbit sauce’; where that name comes from is disputed, from a Madame Lapin to a recipe for rabbit with prunes.

A local brotherhood, the Confrérie du Gay Boulet, has held a contest for the best boulet since 1996.

How to eat it

Cut the boulet in half with your fork and let the fries soak up the sauce. The locals put mayonnaise on the fries too.
