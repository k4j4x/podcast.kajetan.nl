Trappist beer

Trappisttrah-PISTTrappistetrah-PEEST

A Trappist beer is brewed within the walls of a Trappist abbey, by the monks or under their supervision, and the profits go to the community or to charity. Only beers that meet these rules may carry the ‘Authentic Trappist Product’ logo of the International Trappist Association, founded in 1997 to stop others using the name.

Belgium has five Trappist breweries: Chimay (Scourmont Abbey, near Chimay), Rochefort (the Abbey of Notre-Dame de Saint-Remy), Orval, Westmalle and Westvleteren. Achel lost the logo in 2021. Most are strong, bottle-refermented ales; the dubbel style began at Westmalle in 1856. Chimay labels its beers by colour, Rochefort mostly by number (6, 8 and 10).

They are part of Belgian beer culture, which has been on UNESCO’s list of intangible heritage since 2016. You can order them in almost any Belgian café.

How to eat it

It comes with its own glass: pour slowly, and leave the last bit of yeast in the bottle if you prefer a clear beer. Sip it: many Trappists are strong.

How to order

  • Een trappist, alstublieftun trah-PIST, ahl-stew-BLEEFTUne trappiste, s'il vous plaîtewn trah-PEEST, seel voo PLEHA Trappist beer, please

Where to try it

All over Belgium; the abbeys are in Wallonia (Chimay, Rochefort, Orval) and Flanders (Westmalle, Westvleteren)
