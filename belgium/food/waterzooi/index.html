Waterzooi

Gentse waterzooiKHENT-suh WAH-tuhr-zoyWaterzooi à la gantoisewah-tehr-zoy ah lah gah(n)-TWAHZ

Waterzooi is somewhere between a soup and a stew: chicken or fish poached with carrots, leeks, celery and potatoes in a broth that is thickened with egg yolk and cream. The name comes from old Dutch words for water and boiling.

It is Ghent’s own dish. According to Ghent’s tourist office, it was first made with river fish caught in the city; later chicken often took the place of the fish, and today you will find both on the menu. The usual explanation for the switch is that the rivers became too polluted. Emperor Charles V, born in Ghent, is said to have loved it, but that story is hard to pin down.

It is served as a main course in a deep plate, usually with bread.

How to eat it

Eat it with a spoon and a fork: the spoon for the creamy broth, the fork for the chicken and vegetables. Use the bread to mop up the last of the sauce.
