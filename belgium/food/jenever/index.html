Jenever

Jeneveryuh-NAY-vuhrGenièvre / péketzhuh-NYEHVR / pay-KET

Jenever is a grain spirit flavoured with juniper berries, the traditional spirit of Belgium, the Netherlands and nearby areas; the name is a protected geographical indication. ‘Oude’ (old) and ‘jonge’ (young) jenever are not about age but about the recipe: oude jenever contains at least 15% malt wine.

Hasselt calls itself Belgium’s jenever capital. Its National Jenever Museum is housed in a former distillery that in 1975 became the first protected industrial monument in Belgium, and it still distils its own jenever. Every October the town holds the Jeneverfeesten, when the Borrelmanneke fountain runs with jenever.

In Liège the same spirit is called péket. It flows in small glasses at the 15 August festivities in the Outremeuse district, and also at the Fêtes de Wallonie in Namur; mixed with cola it is a ‘blanc-coca’.

How to eat it

It comes in a small glass filled to the brim, young jenever often ice-cold from the freezer. Sip it slowly: it’s strong.

How to order

  • Een jenever, alstublieftun yuh-NAY-vuhr, ahl-stew-BLEEFTUn péket, s'il vous plaîta(n) pay-KET, seel voo PLEHA jenever, please

Where to try it

All over Belgium; Hasselt (Limburg) and Liège above all
