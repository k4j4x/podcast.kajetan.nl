Speculoos

Speculoosspay-kew-LOHSSpéculoosspay-kew-LOHS

Order a coffee in a Belgian café and there is a good chance a small, crunchy, caramel-flavoured biscuit comes with it. That is speculoos, Belgian style: made with caramelised brown sugar and much milder in spice than Dutch speculaas. The Lotus bakery in Lembeke has made it since 1932 and began serving it with coffee in cafés in 1956.

The older, spiced speculoos is pressed into carved wooden moulds and baked in figures, and belongs to the Saint Nicholas season: children get it around 6 December. In 2020 speculoos was added to Brussels’ list of intangible heritage.

Hasselt has its own version, Hasseltse speculaas: thicker, softer and less spiced, and traditionally eaten with a glass of ice-cold jenever. It was once baked only for Saint Nicholas, but has been sold all year since the Second World War.

How to eat it

Dip the biscuit that comes with your coffee into the cup for a second: it softens and tastes even more of caramel. In Hasselt, have yours with a small jenever.
