Cuberdon

Cuberdon / neuzekekew-buhr-DON / NUR-zuh-kuhCuberdonkew-behr-DOH(n)

A cuberdon is a small purple cone, about 2.5 cm across, with a firm crust made with gum arabic and a soft, syrupy centre that tastes of raspberry. In Ghent it is nicknamed neuzeke (‘little nose’) or Gentse neus; in French it is also called a chapeau de curé, ‘priest’s hat’.

Cuberdons only keep for about three weeks before the inside starts to crystallise, which is why they are rarely exported: you really have to eat them in Belgium. Production stopped during the Second World War, when gum arabic could not be had, and started again in 1946. The Flemish agricultural marketing board VLAM recognises it as a regional product.

Tradition says a Ghent pharmacist named De Vynck created it around 1873, but another story points to a clergyman in Bruges. On Ghent’s Groentenmarkt, stalls sell nothing but cuberdons.

How to eat it

Bite off the tip first and be careful: the syrup inside can run. Buy a bag to share, and eat them within a couple of weeks.
