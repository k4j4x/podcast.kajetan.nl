Liège waffle

Luikse wafelLOWK-suh WAH-fulGaufre de Liègegohfr duh LYEZH

The Liège waffle is smaller than the Brussels one, with rounded edges and no corners. It is made from a dense, buttery brioche dough mixed with pearl sugar, which caramelises in the hot iron and gives it crunchy, sticky spots. It is the most common waffle in Belgium, sold by street vendors all over the country and wrapped individually in supermarkets.

It is sweet enough on its own, so it is traditionally eaten plain, without powdered sugar, warm or cold. Some versions have vanilla or cinnamon. In Liège itself people simply call it a gaufre au sucre, ‘sugar waffle’.

A well-loved legend says it was invented in the 18th century by the cook of a prince-bishop of Liège; the Wallonia-Brussels heritage inventory itself calls this a legend. The Liège historian Pierre Leclercq traces it to a recipe published in Liège in 1890.

How to eat it

Eat it from your hand, wrapped in its paper, as you walk. Fresh from the iron the caramelised sugar is very hot, so let it cool for a moment.
