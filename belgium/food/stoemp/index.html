Stoemp

StoempSTOOMPStoempSTOOMP

Stoemp is potatoes mashed together with one or more vegetables: carrots, leeks, spinach, Brussels sprouts, endive or cabbage. The name comes from Brussels dialect ‘doorgestoempte patatjes’, mashed potatoes. Onions softened in butter, thyme and bay leaf give it flavour.

It is a Brussels and Flemish classic; it has been cooked in Brussels since the 19th century, and the city’s tourist office lists it among its specialities. In the Netherlands the same idea is called stamppot.

Stoemp never comes alone: it is served with sausage, black pudding, thick slices of bacon or a fried egg. It is mostly thought of as winter food, but you can find it all year round.

How to eat it

Cut the sausage or bacon into pieces and take a bit of meat and stoemp on every forkful. With a fried egg, break the yolk and let it run into the mash.
