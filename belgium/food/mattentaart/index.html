Mattentaart

Geraardsbergse mattentaartkhuh-RAHRTS-berkh-suh MAH-tuh-tahrtTarte au matontahrt oh mah-TOH(n)

A mattentaart is a round tart of puff pastry, top and bottom, filled with ‘matten’: curds made from fresh raw milk and buttermilk, crumbled and mixed with eggs, sugar and sometimes almond. The result is golden outside and soft, slightly tangy and creamy inside.

It belongs to Geraardsbergen (Grammont in French). The town set quality rules for it in 1665, and since 2007 the Geraardsbergse mattentaart has been an EU protected geographical indication: the milk for the curds must come from Geraardsbergen or neighbouring Lierde. The oldest known recipe is in a cookbook from around 1510.

It is a tart for fairs, birthdays and parties. Every year at the end of April, the town holds a mattentaart day.

How to eat it

Have one with a coffee: eat it from your hand or with a cake fork. It’s at its best fresh from the bakery, on the day it is baked.

How to order

  • Een mattentaartje, alstublieftun MAH-tuh-tahr-chuh, ahl-stew-BLEEFTUne tarte au maton, s'il vous plaîtewn tahrt oh mah-TOH(n), seel voo PLEHA mattentaart, please

Where to try it

Geraardsbergen and Lierde, East Flanders
