Rice tart

RijsttaartRAIST-tahrtTarte au riztahrt oh REE

A tarte au riz is a tart with a base of yeast dough and a thick filling of rice pudding: rice cooked slowly in milk, flavoured with vanilla or cinnamon, left to set for hours and mixed with egg. In the oven the top turns golden with darker spots. It is eaten all over Belgium; in Flanders it is called rijsttaart.

The town that claims it most is Verviers, near Liège, where the name ‘tarte au riz de Verviers’ appears around 1900 and some bakers add crushed macaroons. Local bakers have applied to have it registered as an EU protected geographical indication, and a brotherhood founded in 1990, the Seigneurie de la Vervi-riz, defends it; the claim that the water of the nearby Gileppe dam makes it better is, according to researchers, mostly myth.

It was the tart of Sundays, communions, weddings and village fairs. Today it is mostly eaten in the afternoon with coffee, or as a dessert.

How to eat it

Have a slice with a cup of hot coffee in the afternoon, the way they do in Verviers. A cake fork helps: the filling is soft.
