Gueuze and lambic

GeuzeKHUR-zuhGueuzeGURZ

Lambic is beer fermented without added yeast: the hot wort is left to cool overnight in a wide, open vat, and wild yeasts and bacteria of the Zenne (Senne) valley and the Pajottenland, southwest of Brussels, start the fermentation. That only works in the cool months, roughly October to May. The beer then ages for years in wooden barrels and turns dry and sour.

Gueuze is a blend of young (one-year-old) and older (two- to three-year-old) lambic that ferments again in the bottle, which makes it sparkle. ‘Oude geuze’ (vieille gueuze) must be 100% lambic, and gueuze is protected by the EU as a Traditional Speciality Guaranteed. Kriek is lambic aged with sour cherries.

In Brussels, the Cantillon brewery in Anderlecht, founded in 1900, still brews only lambic and is also the Brussels Gueuze Museum. The lambic brewers and blenders of the region open their doors together for the Toer de Geuze.

How to eat it

Expect sour, dry and fizzy, not sweet: sip it slowly, like a wine. If it’s too sour for you, try a kriek next.
