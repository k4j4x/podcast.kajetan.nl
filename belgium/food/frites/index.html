Fries

FrietFREETFritesFREET

Belgian fries are fried twice: first to cook the potato through, then a second time to make them crisp. The traditional frying fat is beef fat. You buy them at a frietkot or friterie, a chip shop or roadside stand; there are about 5,000 of them in Belgium, and frietkot culture has been on Flanders’ list of intangible heritage since 2014.

They come in a paper cone or tray with a spoonful of sauce on top. In the 19th century fries came with stewed onions and vinegar, pickles sauce or mustard; mayonnaise caught on in the early 20th century, once it was sold ready-made (from 1920), and it is still the favourite, though every frietkot has a long list of other sauces. In Flanders you order ‘een pakje’ (a pack) with the sauce of your choice.

Who invented fries is contested. A popular Belgian story says that people in the Meuse valley fried potatoes when the frozen river kept them from fishing, but it rests on a manuscript nobody has ever produced, and some historians think fries came from Paris. In Bruges, the Frietmuseum is devoted to their history.

How to eat it

Use the little fork that comes with them and dip each fry in the sauce. Eat them on the spot, while they’re hot: fries go soft quickly.
