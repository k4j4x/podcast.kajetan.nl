Paying

Belgians mostly pay electronically, and the name you’ll see everywhere is Bancontact, Belgium’s own payment system, which handles billions of payments a year. Credit cards aren’t accepted everywhere, though: the supermarket chain Colruyt, famously, doesn’t take them. So carry a little cash as a back-up.

Cash is protected by law. Since 31 March 2024, shops and other businesses must accept cash when you pay in person. They may only refuse it in specific cases, for example for safety reasons announced at the entrance or for a banknote out of all proportion to what you’re buying, and cash payments are capped at €3,000. When you pay in cash, the total is rounded to the nearest 5 cents.

At many tills you’ll also see a sticker with a QR code. You can scan it with the Bancontact Pay app (until 2026 called Payconiq by Bancontact) or with Wero, a European payment service backed by a group of banks.
