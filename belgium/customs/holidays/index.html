Holidays & festivals

Belgium has ten public holidays: New Year’s Day, Easter Monday, 1 May, Ascension Day, Whit Monday, 21 July, 15 August, 1 November, 11 November and Christmas Day. 21 July is National Day, and 11 November is Armistice Day, remembering the end of the First World War. Opening hours can differ on these days, so check before you go.

Don’t be fooled by the community days: 11 July for the Flemish Community, 27 September for the French Community and 15 November for the German-speaking Community, which is also the King’s Feast. They are not official public holidays and employers don’t have to give a day off. The community’s own civil servants do get it, though, and on the King’s Feast all public administrations close, so a town hall may be shut.

Several festivals are on UNESCO’s list of intangible heritage, including these two. The Carnival of Binche in Wallonia fills the three days before Lent and peaks on Shrove Tuesday, when the Gilles in their wax masks dance on the Grand-Place. And in early July the Ommegang, a historical procession that goes back to 1348, parades through Brussels to the Grand-Place over two evenings.
