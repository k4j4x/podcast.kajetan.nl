Beer culture

In Belgium, beer is officially culture: in 2016 UNESCO added Belgian beer culture to its list of intangible heritage. More than 1,500 types of beer are made here, and serving them is taken seriously. Every beer has its own branded glass, and pouring one into the wrong glass is considered a real faux pas.

Five Belgian abbeys brew Trappist beer: Westmalle, Westvleteren, Chimay, Orval and Rochefort. Achel used to be the sixth, until its last monks left. Many Belgian beers are much stronger than an ordinary lager, with some Trappists reaching 11%, so take your time: Belgian beers are made for savouring.

Southwest of Brussels, in the Pajottenland and the Zenne valley, brewers make lambic, fermented spontaneously by wild yeasts, and gueuze, a blend of young and old lambic. Just want a simple lager? In Flanders, ask for een pintje, a small glass of draught pils. And look out for the brown cafés: old, cosy pubs, and in specialised beer cafés the list can run into the hundreds.
