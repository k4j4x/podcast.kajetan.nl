Comics country

Belgium takes comics seriously: here they’re called the ‘ninth art’. Tintin, created by Hergé, first appeared in January 1929 and set the style for decades of Franco-Belgian comics. The Smurfs by Peyo and Lucky Luke by Morris are Belgian too, and Spirou and Gaston Lagaffe come from the Belgian magazine Spirou. In Flanders, generations grew up with Suske en Wiske by Willy Vandersteen.

In Brussels, comics are part of the street scene. Since 1991, murals of comic heroes have been appearing on the city’s walls, and there are now more than 80. Follow the comic strip trail from visit.brussels to find them as you walk around town.

For the full story, head to the Comic Art Museum on Rue des Sables in the centre of Brussels. It’s set in the former Waucquez warehouse, an Art Nouveau building designed by Victor Horta and completed in 1906, and it’s run by the Belgian Comic Strip Center, founded in 1984.
