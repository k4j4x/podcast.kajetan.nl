Three languages

Belgium has three official languages, and where you are decides which one people speak. Flanders in the north speaks Dutch (often called Flemish), Wallonia in the south speaks French, and nine municipalities in the east form the small German-speaking Community. Brussels is officially bilingual, French and Dutch, but French is the dominant language on its streets.

The borders between these language areas were fixed by law in 1962–63, after decades of quarrelling, and they still matter. In Flanders, opening a conversation in French can come across as insensitive. Start in Dutch, even if it’s just ‘goeiedag’, or simply in English: it’s a neutral bridge and widely spoken.

In Wallonia, French is the language to use. English is less common there than in Flanders: in a 2006 study about half of the Flemish said they knew English, against fewer than one in five Walloons. A few words of French go a long way.
