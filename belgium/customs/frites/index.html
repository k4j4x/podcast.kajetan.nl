Frites

Frites are a Belgian institution, and the classic place to buy them is the frietkot (in French friterie or fritkot): a simple chip shop, often family-run, where it doesn’t matter who you are. Flanders put frietkot culture on its heritage inventory in 2014, and by July 2017 it had been officially recognised as part of Belgium’s intangible heritage by several bodies.

Every frietkot has its own style, but there are standards. Walloon friteries with the ‘Friterie de chez nous’ quality label cut their frites 1 cm thick, fry them first at 150 °C and then at 175 °C, and serve them in a cone or a carton tray, to eat on the spot or take away. Traditionally, Belgian frites are fried in beef fat.

Then comes the big decision: the sauce. Mayonnaise is the classic, but the list at the counter is long, with names like andalouse, brazil, tzigane or mammouth.
