Two names per town

Many Belgian towns have two names, and sometimes three. Liège is Luik in Dutch, Mons is Bergen, Namur is Namen and Tournai is Doornik, while French speakers call Antwerpen Anvers and Brugge Bruges. In the German-speaking east, Liège becomes Lüttich. People generally use the name in their own language.

Signs follow the region you are in, not the place you are heading to. On a motorway in Flanders you follow Luik; cross into Wallonia and the same city becomes Liège. Trains work the same way: a train from Ostend to Liège is announced in Dutch as going to ‘Luik’ while it’s in Flanders, and in French as going to ‘Liège’ once it reaches Wallonia. Tip: look up both names of where you’re going before you set off.

Brussels is the only officially bilingual region. Every street sign shows French and Dutch, a rule that goes back to mayor Charles Buls in the 19th century. So Rue des Sables and Zandstraat are the same street, and train announcements there come in both languages.
